What this system does

An always-on trading assistant that runs on a home computer: it keeps market data fresh, builds a watchlist from a filter you choose, buys under cash rules, and protects open positions with stop orders — paper-trading first until someone chooses to go live.

How it runs

The bot lives on a personal computer (or a small always-on machine at home). Passwords, Schwab login tokens, and the trading database stay on that machine. This page is only a window into it.

A scheduler wakes on a loop during the US stock session and walks the same pipeline for each account: refresh data, rebuild the watchlist, consider buys, then check stops and sells.

  1. 1. Set up an account

    On Actions, each person connects their own Schwab account and sets a few guardrails. Until those are set, the bot will not trade.

    • Schwab link — so the bot can see cash, positions, and (later) place orders.
    • Cash floor — never buy if the purchase would leave cash below this amount. Sells still run.
    • Account-value floor — no new buys while the whole account (cash + positions) is below this. Sells still run.
    • Buy size — how many dollars to spend on each new stock (whole shares only; it will not add to a name already owned).
    • Watchlist filter — Safe Giant, Risky Momentum, or a custom recipe. That filter is the only source of new buys.
    • Dry-run, then Go live — it starts by logging what it would do. Real Schwab orders wait until someone presses Go live.

  2. 2. Market data from Yahoo

    Fundamentals for the US stock universe come from Yahoo Finance — things like price, market cap, P/E, beta, short interest, dividend yield, and analyst targets. That database is shared: every account reads the same numbers.

    A background job works through the oldest or missing tickers first (about 150 per hour), skips names already updated today, and keeps the universe within a few days of fresh. The cards above are that shared snapshot.

  3. 3. Filter into a watchlist

    The chosen filter is applied to that Yahoo data. Names that fail a rule drop out; the rest are ranked (usually by analyst upside — how far the consensus target sits above the current price). The result is the watchlist: the only list the bot is allowed to buy from. Built-in recipes are in the table below; a custom filter can be built on Actions.

  4. 4. Buys

    During regular hours (9:30–4:00 p.m. New York), the bot looks at the top of the watchlist and, when cash and account-value floors allow, places one buy of the chosen size. It will not pick random tickers, will not buy after hours, and in dry-run it only writes a log of what it would have sent to Schwab.

  5. 5. Stop limits

    After a buy, the bot does not arm a broker stop on the same calendar day (that would risk a same-day round trip). Starting the next trading day, a protective STOP_LIMIT sits at Schwab so the position is covered even if the home machine is briefly offline.

    • Trailing stop — once a position is up enough from cost, the stop follows the peak down by a buffer; it only ratchets up, never down.
    • Tighter off-watchlist — if the name later fails the filter, the buffer and hard floor tighten.
    • Hard stop — a last-resort floor versus the purchase price if a trail never armed, or the thesis is gone.

  6. 6. Sells

    About every 15 minutes while the market is open, a sell check looks at enrolled positions: ratchet trails, honor hard stops, and update the resting Schwab order. Holdings marked as legacy (owned before the algorithm) are left alone until they are enrolled. Buys can be paused without turning off this sell protection.

Where to look

  • Trader — positions, open orders, the live watchlist, and performance versus the S&P 500.
  • Events — a log of buys, sells, stops, and watchlist changes.
  • Actions — Schwab connection, floors and buy size, filter choice, Run / Pause / Go live.
